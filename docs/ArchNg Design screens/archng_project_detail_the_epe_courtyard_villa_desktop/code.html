The Epe Courtyard Villa

A climate-responsive monolithic rammed-earth sanctuary balancing oceanfront breezes and artisanal Nigerian craft along the tidal shores of Lagos.

Design Narrative & Climate Strategy

ARCON Monograph Index #EP-402

Conceived as an antidote to standard Lagos concrete-block construction, the Epe Courtyard Villa reimagines vernacular West African building traditions through modern structural precision. Situated on the fragile mangrove-adjacent perimeter of the Epe Lagoon, the residence engages the humid coastal climate with a passive cooling architecture that virtually eliminates daytime dependence on mechanical refrigeration.

The external envelope is formed entirely of 450mm load-bearing stabilized rammed-earth walls. Excavated directly from local laterite deposits within a 15-kilometer radius, the composite subsoil is blended with 6% mineral binder and crushed quarry aggregates. This monumental thermal mass acts as a natural heat capacitor, soaking up intense midday solar radiance and dissipating it gradually across the cooler nocturnal hours.

“The villa does not fight the tropical Atlantic microclimate; it breathes with the thermal tides of the Epe lagoon.”
— Adaeze Okafor, Principal Architect (Adaeze Okafor Studio)

A central atrium acts as a low-pressure thermal chimney. As offshore breeze sweeps over the lagoon pool, evaporative cooling draws chilled air through operable Western Nigerian teak louvers situated at ground level. Warmer interior air rises naturally through 6.2-meter double-height living volumes and exhausts through high-level clerestory brise-soleil, establishing perpetual cross-ventilation.
